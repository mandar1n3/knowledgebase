# Flow charts

> A flow chart is a type of diagram that represents a workflow or process.
> It can also be defined as a diagrammatic representation of an algorithm.

For making a our diagrams we will be using [flowgorithm](http://flowgorithm.org/index.html).
It's a free beginner frendly programming language based on graphical flowcharts.

- This will cover 4 shapes of a flow chart.
    - A diamond: representing a conditional expression.
    - A square: reprsenting an initialisation or assignement of a variable.
    - A parallelogram: representing i/o.
    - A hexagon: representing a loop.
- A flow chart is a universal language making it easy to understand by anyone.
- There are a number of finete operations.
- The direction of flow chart is always start -> finish. The arrows should
  converge towards the end.
- The data that will we use, will be comming at the moment of execution.
- A flow chart has to produce a result.
- The operations have to be preciscly described inorder to produce the
  expected result.
- There are commun types of errors that can produce an erroneous are:
    - A misanalysis of the problem domain.
    - A bad implementation of the algorithm.
    - Mistranslation of the algorithm into a specific programming language.
    - In rare cases a computer failure.
- By convention the CamelCase notation is used.
